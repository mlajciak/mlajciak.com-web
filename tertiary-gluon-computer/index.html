THEORETICAL CONCEPT OF TERNARY GLUON-BASED COMPUTER

The ternary register considered here is the color degree of freedom of quantum chromodynamics, and the gates are gluons. The gauge group is \(SU(N_c)\) with \(N_c = 3\). A quark transforms in the fundamental representation \(\mathbf{3}\). An antiquark transforms in \(\bar{\mathbf{3}}\). A one-particle color state is a vector in a three-dimensional space, so the color of a quark is a trit.

A gluon transforms in the adjoint representation. A color together with an anticolor decomposes as

\[\mathbf{3}\otimes\bar{\mathbf{3}} = \mathbf{8}\oplus\mathbf{1}.\]

The dimension of the adjoint follows at once,

\[\dim\mathrm{adj}\,SU(3) = N_c^2 - 1 = 8.\]

The singlet on the right-hand side of the decomposition is not a gluon. Each of the eight generators changes the color of a quark, or mixes the three colors while remaining traceless. The stored symbol is the quark color. The gluon is the operation on that symbol.

Asymptotic states are color singlets. A state of nonzero color charge is connected to a compensating charge by a flux tube. The static energy of the tube is linear in its length,

\[V(L) = \sigma L .\]

The phenomenological string tension and its conversion with \(\hbar c = 0.197\,\mathrm{GeV\,fm}\) are

\[\sigma \simeq 0.18\,\mathrm{GeV}^2 \simeq 0.91\,\mathrm{GeV/fm}.\]

A separation of one fermi therefore stores

\[V(1\,\mathrm{fm}) \simeq 0.91\,\mathrm{GeV} = 1.46\times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J}.\]

The cost has no constant term that could be engineered away: it grows with \(L\). A tube of nuclear length hadronizes into color-singlet states. Net color is not a stable cell.

The lightest color-singlet state made only of gluons is the scalar glueball. Lattice spectroscopy places its mass at

\[m_{0^{++}} \simeq 1.7\,\mathrm{GeV} = 2.72\times 10^{-10}\,\mathrm{J}.\]

That mass is the energy required to create the smallest gluonic cell. The Landauer energy of one trit at temperature \(T\) is

\[E_L = k_B T \ln 3 .\]

At \(T = 300\,\mathrm{K}\),

\[E_L = 4.55\times 10^{-21}\,\mathrm{J},\]

and the ratio of the cell energy to that floor is

\[\frac{m_{0^{++}}}{E_L} \simeq 6.0\times 10^{10}.\]

Above the crossover, color is not confined, and the carrier is a gluon plasma. An ideal gas of gluons has

\[g_* = 2(N_c^2 - 1) = 16\]

degrees of freedom and energy density

\[\varepsilon = \frac{\pi^2}{30}\, g_*\, T^4 .\]

Lattice QCD places the crossover at \(T_c \simeq 156\,\mathrm{MeV}\). Substitution of that temperature gives

\[\varepsilon(T_c) \simeq 0.41\,\mathrm{GeV/fm}^3 .\]

A cell of volume \(1\,\mathrm{fm}^3\) still costs a fraction of a GeV. The lifetime of the hot region in a nuclear collision is of order \(10\,\mathrm{fm}/c \simeq 3\times 10^{-23}\,\mathrm{s}\), after which the plasma hadronizes.

The dynamical time is fixed by the QCD scale \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}} \simeq 200\,\mathrm{MeV}\),

\[\tau = \frac{\hbar}{\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}} \simeq 3.3\times 10^{-24}\,\mathrm{s}.\]

One gluon exchange and the decay width of a glueball resonance are both set by this scale. Storage time and gate time are the same time, so an error-correcting cycle has no interval in which to run.

A detector couples to singlets. The baryon singlet and the meson singlet are

\[\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\,\epsilon_{ijk}\,\lvert q^{i} q^{j} q^{k}\rangle , \qquad \delta^{i}_{\;j}\,\lvert q^{j}\bar{q}_{i}\rangle .\]

Both projectors sum over the color index. The ternary label is not an eigenvalue of the state that reaches the detector.

The construction is fixed by the relations above. There are eight gluonic gates acting on a three-color trit. The minimum energy of a legal cell is \(m_{0^{++}} \simeq 1.7\,\mathrm{GeV}\), about \(6\times 10^{10}\) Landauer energies of one trit at room temperature, or \(\sigma \simeq 0.91\,\mathrm{GeV/fm}\) for a flux tube. The cell and the gate both last about \(10^{-24}\,\mathrm{s}\). The readout alphabet has one symbol. Below \(\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}\) the trit is not an asymptotic degree of freedom. Above it, the carrier has energy density of order \(1\,\mathrm{GeV/fm}^3\).
